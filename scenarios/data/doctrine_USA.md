# Revealed Military Doctrine — United States

US doctrine is **air-superiority-first, stand-off-precision, ISR-saturated, coalition-coordinated, casualty-averse.** The American way of war as actually practiced since 1991 is to dominate the electromagnetic spectrum and the air, then employ precision long-range fires with extensive ISR support, and only commit ground forces under conditions of overmatch. Mass infantry assault and acceptance of high friendly casualties are doctrinal anti-patterns.

For a Western Pacific peer contingency, the US operational concept is built around AirSea Battle (2010–2015), its successor Joint Concept for Access and Maneuver in the Global Commons (JAM-GC), and INDOPACOM's Pacific Deterrence Initiative — emphasizing distributed maritime operations, dispersed lethality, contested logistics, and the protection of forward bases against PRC missile salvos. This is a different operational concept than the COIN paradigm that consumed US doctrine 2003–2014, but the *style* (process slowness, casualty aversion, coalition-seeking, ISR dependency) carries forward.

## Revealed Patterns

1. **Air superiority is the precondition for everything else.** Achieved by D+1 to D+3 in Desert Storm (1991) and OIF (2003); without it, ground operations are not initiated. In the Pacific peer context, this becomes *contested air superiority* — air denial rather than dominance — and US doctrine has been reorganized around that constraint (e.g. Agile Combat Employment for the USAF, Distributed Maritime Operations for the USN).

2. **Stand-off precision over direct contact.** Tomahawk, JASSM-ER, LRASM, B-21 / B-2 stand-off, and CSG carrier air wings strike from outside the inner threat ring. Direct close engagement is the last resort, not the first instrument. Libya 2011 was the cleanest expression: no US ground forces, ~7 months of stand-off airpower in coalition.

3. **ISR-saturated battlespace; never fight blind.** OEF, OIF, Syria CT 2014–2019, Libya 2011 — all conducted under persistent ISR coverage (Predator/Reaper, Global Hawk, JSTARS, RC-135, U-2, space-based). When ISR is degraded (e.g., contested EM in a Pacific peer fight), US tempo slows dramatically. The institutional default in low-information conditions is to *delay action* rather than act blind.

4. **SOF + airpower as the preferred force package.** OEF Phase 1 (October–December 2001): ~300 SOF + CIA SAD + B-52 close air support + Northern Alliance proxy = Taliban regime collapse in 78 days. This template was reused in Libya 2011 and Syria CT 2014–2019. Mass conventional infantry is reserved for situations where SOF + air cannot achieve the objective.

5. **COIN reluctance, learned hard.** Iraq 2003–2011 transformed US doctrine. FM 3-24 (Petraeus/Mattis, 2006) institutionalized population-centric counterinsurgency, but the cost in casualties, time, and political capital permanently soured US strategic culture on extended ground occupation. Post-Afghanistan, the US assumption is that any contingency requiring sustained ground COIN is a failure of pre-conflict policy.

6. **Coalition-seeking is structural, not optional.** Even "unilateral" US operations (Libya 2011, Syria 2017 cruise strikes, OEF 2001) sought coalition cover before kinetic action. Desert Storm 1991 had 35 coalition members. OIF 2003 had ~40, including the much-criticized "coalition of the willing." Pattern: US would rather act with thin coalition cover than truly alone.

7. **Casualty aversion is load-bearing.** Mogadishu 1993 (18 KIA) prompted withdrawal from Somalia within months. OEF/OIF KIA accumulated slowly enough to sustain operations, but post-2011 the political tolerance for new high-casualty operations is near-zero. This bias is exploitable — adversaries who can credibly threaten meaningful US KIA can deter US action. In a Pacific peer fight, the potential loss of a CSG (~5,000 personnel on a carrier alone) is the central political constraint.

8. **NCA process slowness for novel scenarios.** Third Taiwan Strait Crisis (1995–96): CSG deployment authorization took weeks through the NSC Principals Committee process. The chain runs President → SECDEF/CJCS → INDOPACOM → CSG commander. Standing orders and pre-authorized response options exist (e.g., FONOPs), but anything novel hits the 24–72 hour deliberation cycle. PRC and others are aware of this and may attempt fait accompli before US can authorize response.

9. **Declaratory deterrence before force deployment.** Senkaku/Diaoyu 2010–2013: US publicly stated Article 5 covers the islands but did not deploy additional forces. Pelosi visit 2022: US did not cancel but also did not send CSG into the Strait during PRC exercises. Pattern: US prefers public signaling and arms transfers to forward force deployment, escalating to forces only when signaling is insufficient.

10. **Sanctions and economic coercion before kinetic action.** Russia 2014 (Crimea), Russia 2022 (Ukraine), Iran 2018+, North Korea continuous — US prefers economic instruments and will accept years of "below-the-threshold" non-resolution rather than employ force.

## Operational Tempo

**Slow to escalate, decisive when threshold is clearly crossed.** Pre-conflict deliberation is measured in days to weeks. Once forces are committed, tempo accelerates but is gated by ISR collection, target development, ROE review, and political authorization. The US does not improvise high-stakes kinetic operations on a 24-hour cycle. Adversaries who compress decision timelines (e.g. fait accompli) are exploiting this.

## Rules of Engagement

**Restrictive by international standard.** US ROE emphasize positive identification, proportionality, collateral damage estimation (CDE), and authorization-level escalation (e.g., dynamic targeting requires O-6+ approval in most theaters, civilian-density targets require GO/FO+). The standing-ROE-to-mission-ROE pipeline is documented and bureaucratic. Even in declared conflict, the US does not engage targets it cannot positively identify.

## Anti-Patterns (Doctrinally Inconsistent)

- **Mass casualty force-on-force without coalition cover.** US has not done this since Vietnam.
- **First-use of nuclear weapons in a conventional crisis.** Crosses every doctrinal firebreak.
- **Strikes on adversary homeland infrastructure (PRC mainland) absent direct attack on US forces.** Crosses PRC's stated red line and US's own implicit one.
- **Direct US ground combat in defense of Taiwan absent PRC amphibious assault.** Pre-positions trip-wire forces, does not commit large ground formations.
- **Acting unilaterally over allied objection (specifically Japan in WestPac).** US is treaty-bound to consult Japan before triggering Article 5 obligations.
